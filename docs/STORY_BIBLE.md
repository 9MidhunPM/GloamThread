# Gloamthread: Two Ends — story bible

Version 2.0 · Planning content. Implement the Phase 1 beats first; the full campaign is proposed later work.

## Story in one breath

Two couriers inherit opposite ends of the last living thread. One remembers a kingdom of furnaces and brass; the other remembers roots and rain. To bring a stranded district home, they must repair the same broken path without deciding that only one of their memories deserves to survive.

## Tone and experience

Aim for quiet wonder, odd little inhabitants, danger with readable causes, and warmth earned through action. Ruins should feel inhabited by people who improvised their way through disaster. A broken tea kettle can become a bell; a stranded delivery can matter more than an abstract prophecy.

Let humour interrupt solemnity occasionally. Avoid constant quips, relentless misery, lore lectures, and an imitation of another game's dialogue. Restoration should feel hopeful without making the loss imaginary.

The player should learn the premise by crossing a bridge together, not by reading three screens of creation mythology.

## The Weftlands

The Weftlands were joined by living seams: paths woven between settlements, maintained by small communal looms. A seam held more than stone together. It preserved the names people gave a place, its familiar light, and the memory of how to return.

The central loom gradually imposed one approved pattern on every district. Paths became efficient, uniform, and brittle. When that pattern failed, the connections unravelled. People call the advancing darkness the **night tide**.

Each courier's spool retains a different interpretation of the world. Tavi's Ember thread remembers the labour and warmth of old machines. Neri's Moss thread remembers growth that continued between them. These are visual perspectives on one material place, never separate collision realities.

The night tide creates narrative urgency but no campaign countdown. Players may explore, experiment, fail, and recover without permanently losing residents.

## Characters

### Tavi — the Ember courier

- Role: a practical repair courier who believes a problem becomes manageable once something is moving again.
- Appearance: ash hood, muted teal mantle, brass spool, amber lantern, hooked shuttle. Original silhouette; no horned mask.
- Combat: basic slash and Ember Arc, a charged cleave with extra stagger; starting target 5 hearts.
- Inner problem: rushing to fix things can mean deciding for everyone else what “fixed” means.
- Relationship: initially assumes Neri is hesitant; learns that paying attention is also work.
- Voice: short, concrete, occasionally dry. Tavi offers to carry a difficult thing before admitting it is frightening.

### Neri — the Moss courier

- Role: a patient path recorder who notices the small signs people leave for those following them.
- Appearance: leaf-shaped stitched hood, plum scarf, round moss-green spool, needle baton.
- Combat: basic slash and Spore Bolt, a short projectile with brief normal-enemy slow; starting target 4 hearts.
- Inner problem: preserving every possibility can become an excuse to avoid choosing a path.
- Relationship: initially sees Tavi as careless; learns that an imperfect repair can give someone a chance to get home.
- Voice: observant, warm, occasionally mischievous. Neri notices an odd detail and then makes its significance clear.

Both are full protagonists. Neither is the designated leader, healer, passenger, or comic assistant. Either can activate objectives, revive the other, stagger the boss, and complete Cross-stitch.

### Oru — keeper of Lantern Rest

- The Phase 1 slice's only named NPC.
- Tends the lantern checkpoint and remembers who has not crossed home.
- Gives a concrete objective: restore the Lantern Loom so the stranded district has a return path.
- Knows enough to guide; withholds no basic controls or vital progression rule for the sake of mystery.
- Visual idea: a broad woven rain cape, tiny copper spectacles, a lantern staff patched with different woods.
- Oru is capable and tired, with a sense of humour. Avoid the disposable mentor cliché.

### The Spoolwarden — a duty that outlived its instructions

- A wooden loom tender dragging a broken shuttle under a cracked bell.
- Still follows an old rule: no passage while the district's pattern is inconsistent.
- The couriers' different spools make it recognise a fault and attack.
- Its intent is shown through posture, warning bell, blocked passage and repeated pattern, not a long speech.
- Defeat releases the trapped Threadstep pattern. It collapses into a quiet repairable mechanism; no graphic death is needed.
- The slice ends before any later recovery quest. Do not add another NPC or boss to resolve this thread.

## Phase 1: The First Shared Stitch

Target first clear: 15–20 minutes, to be measured and revised. Dialogue below is draft original text; retain its purpose when editing for pacing.

### r00 — Lantern Rest: something worth returning to

The two couriers arrive on opposite sides of a dormant communal loom. Split screen immediately shows the same settlement differently. Tavi sees copper ribs and warm ash. Neri sees roots holding the ribs upright and little pale fungi beneath the benches.

Let players move and jump before any dialogue. The dark bridge home remains visible in both views. Oru stands beside the checkpoint lantern.

Draft exchange:

> Oru: “The far district is still there. The way home isn't.”
>
> Tavi: “Then we give it one.”
>
> Neri: “Both ends this time.”

Oru names the Lantern Loom and points toward the Atrium. Checkpoint activation warms the lantern, heals both couriers, and establishes the safe return sound.

### r01 — Mossfoot Atrium: a promise the player can remember

Build a distinctive bell-shaped tree around an old brass arch. This landmark must remain recognisable in both themes.

Players see the Severed Causeway branch, clearly beyond their current jump. A stitched dash symbol and map marker record it without opening it. The forward route toward the Warden is readable.

Optional one-line observation:

> Neri: “That gap kept its landing. It lost the part before it.”

No jump exploit, damage boost or secret special is required to cross early. Let the blocked path remain an understandable question.

### r02 — Spindle Steps: discover what two people make possible

Use safe platforms to teach jumping and split-screen orientation. Two anchor points sit on separately reachable ledges.

The first activated anchor latches with a visible half-pattern. When the partner activates the other, both screens show one bridge being woven into existence. It can appear as braided metal strands in Ember and root fibres in Moss, with identical edges.

Draft line after completion:

> Tavi: “I thought you were holding the other end.”
>
> Neri: “I was. You finally stopped pulling.”

The joke rewards the action. It must not pause a dangerous jump or imply the decorative thread has rope physics.

### r03 — Glasswing Gallery: become a useful partner

Introduce Spoolmites first, then Wickwings. Let a safe first encounter expose the tells before combining them. Teach both specials through situations where they help, without making either compulsory.

A side pocket contains the shared lantern charm. It is found through a readable clue, such as an unlit bell-shaped recess; no random wall punching. Collecting it changes both lanterns cosmetically and produces one shared reward sound.

Allow play to create the revive story. Do not script a mandatory downing or repeatedly interrupt with rescue tutorials.

### r04 — Warden's Landing: breathe before the demand

Checkpoint B sits where the broken bell is visible but the boss cannot reach. Lower music density, restore health, show the next doorway and a clear arena warning.

Oru does not physically appear here; preserve the one-NPC budget. A small carved instruction can read:

> “Keep a thread for the one behind you.”

This reinforces revival and partnership without revealing boss timing.

### r05 — Broken Bell: turn difference into coordination

The Spoolwarden recognises the two spools, tolls once, and bars the arena. Telegraph sweep, ground strike and charge with distinct silhouettes and recovery windows.

A stagger exposes its bright seam. If the other courier hits it, Cross-stitch produces a satisfying shared burst. It is an optional advantage; the encounter remains fair when players do not discover it.

Victory quiets the bell. Both couriers gain Threadstep together, with a short safe practice strip. The shortcut back to the Atrium opens visibly.

Draft exchange:

> Tavi: “It was keeping the road safe.”
>
> Neri: “From everyone who needed it.”

Leave the mechanism resting, rather than forcing a moral choice or long explanation.

### r06 — Severed Causeway: answer the earlier question

The familiar Atrium landmark makes the return legible. Players recognise the old gap, try their new dash safely, then cross the real causeway route.

The second anchor pair restores a crossing with a larger visual payoff. It uses the same interaction grammar as r02, with more dramatic scenery rather than a new synchronisation requirement.

Both themes show the same thread structure becoming solid. The distant lanterns begin responding.

### r07 — Lantern Loom: let the action finish the story

The two couriers activate opposite loom stations. Activation can latch; it does not demand simultaneous button presses. Their two thread colours enter one machine while remaining distinct.

The loom restores the route home. Lights travel back through the authored rooms. Show a short, skippable environmental sequence and a safe return route to r00. Residents can be distant noninteractive silhouettes; no additional named NPC is required.

Oru's return line:

> “Different roads. Same promise. Welcome home.”

The ending card names the completed objective, notes the optional charm if found, and offers continue, new game with swapped fixed characters, or menu. Swapping characters is replay flavour, not a second campaign.

## Environmental storytelling rules

- Every room contains one dominant landmark; small detail supports it.
- Shared geometry must tell the same practical truth in both visual themes.
- A repair changes something observable: a route, a light chain, a bell's sound or returning movement in the distance.
- Decorative text is brief and optional. Required information appears through play and clear objective prompts.
- Save/loading or skipping dialogue never removes required progress or repeats a unique reward.
- Give the couriers a few reaction lines; do not comment on every kill, jump or pickup.
- Use silence before a boss and layered music after restoration. Do not play two theme soundtracks simultaneously.

## Full campaign: proposed 96-room arc

The budget is Lantern Rest with 6 rooms plus six regions of 15 rooms each. Boss arenas, rest areas, shortcuts and secrets count inside those allocations. The eight MVP rooms become part of that world; they are not eight extra rooms.

| Area | Rooms | Story purpose | Playful or emotional payoff |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| Lantern Rest | 6 | Reconnecting hub whose residents disagree kindly about what should return | New routes and returning activity make progress visible |
| Wickerreach | 15 | Restore the first district; learn that looms can preserve different memories | First bridge, Spoolwarden, Threadstep, an expanded orchard-bell finale |
| Ashloom Kilns | 15 | Meet the consequences of repairs made without listening | Heat mechanisms, vertical routes and choosing which communal machine to restart first |
| Bellglass Mire | 15 | Learn how a recorded story changes when someone else repeats it | Resonant glass, reflected scenery and cooperative timing with generous recovery |
| Pale Archive | 15 | Discover records deliberately reduced to one authorised pattern | Reopen erased routes and recover names through exploration rather than lore dumps |
| Rootbound Foundry | 15 | Reveal that growth and machinery already depend on each other | Combined traversal, machines repaired with living material, a major hub return |
| Nightweft Crown | 15 | Reach the central loom and reject the demand for one perfect perspective | Final synthesis of learned rules; two patterns remain visible in the restored world |

The central antagonist is a rigid preservation process called **the Measure**, not an evil version of either courier. It mistakes disagreement for damage. Its late-game form and final encounter are proposals to prototype after the slice, not additional MVP content.

The final resolution repairs a network capable of holding differences. Tavi accepts that a repair needs the people who use it. Neri accepts that preserving possibilities sometimes requires choosing a path now. Their individual arcs resolve through successful shared actions.

Keep campaign decisions local and visible. Avoid promising a combinatorial branching story with dozens of endings. One complete ending with optional regional acknowledgements is sufficient for the larger plan.

## Integrating future custom characters

Phase 2 introduces new couriers whose spools translate their self-portraits into an appearance and visual theme. Tavi and Neri remain the fixed default pair and original campaign leads.

Custom avatars can occupy the two courier roles without requiring every generated biography to rewrite the plot. Their chosen profile controls an allowed kit, bounded stats and cosmetics; required team traversal and the canonical map stay intact.

The theme promise is “a different way to see this place.” It does not imply different platforms, hidden lethal hazards, unique mandatory maps or dynamically invented quests.

## Story acceptance

- A fresh pair can explain the immediate objective after the opening without reading this document.
- Players recognise at least one earlier place after unlocking Threadstep.
- Either player can identify a moment their partner materially helped.
- The ending clearly shows that the district's return path was restored.
- Dialogue never blocks recovery, obscures a telegraph, or makes the second player wait through long monologues.
- The full campaign premise is suggested by the slice, while the local objective resolves completely.
